# Newsletter Growth

## What this does
Takes a newsletter and builds the system to grow it: the offer that convinces someone to hand over an email, the loops that turn readers into more readers, the publishing rhythm that keeps opens high, and the metrics that tell you whether the audience is actually engaged or just large. It builds the growth plan. It does not write every issue for you.

## What you'll need
You do not need to connect anything to get value today. Bring your topic and the skill runs now. Connect the tools below and it reads your real open, click, and growth numbers instead of guessing.

- Works today with: your topic, who it is for, how often you publish, and roughly how many subscribers you have. Paste it in.
- More powerful connected to an email or newsletter tool: it reads subscribers, opens, clicks, and unsubscribes, so the plan targets your real weak spot, not a generic one.
- Sharper with a web-analytics tool: shows which pages and referrers drive signups, so you double down on the sources that actually convert.
- Sharper with a CRM: ties subscribers to pipeline, so the newsletter is measured on business impact, not applause.

## How this runs at your connection level
This skill is never reliant on a connector. It runs on what you tell it today and gets more powerful as you connect tools. It never invents an open rate it cannot see. A missing metric is a prompt, not a guess.

- **Bring your data**: paste the topic, cadence, and rough size. The skill builds the subscribe offer, the growth loops, the content rhythm, and the metric set today. No connection required.
- **Connect your tools**: the same skill reads your real opens, clicks, and growth curve and points the plan at the actual bottleneck (acquisition, activation, or retention).
- **Just exploring**: no newsletter yet? Get the framework, the exact inputs it reads, and a worked example, so you can see the whole system before you send issue one.

Every run ends with the one thing that would make the next run sharper, a metric to track or a tool to connect.

## Customize this for yourself
This was built for a B2B operator growing a topic-focused newsletter tied to a business. Set these to your stack:

| Set this | What it is | Default / Example |

| TOPIC | what the newsletter is about | your specific angle, not a broad category |
| AUDIENCE | who it is for | the reader you want, by role |
| CADENCE | how often you publish | weekly, biweekly |

| GROWTH_LOOPS | how readers bring readers | referrals, shares, cross-promos |

The rates and counts below are illustrative placeholders, not benchmarks. Replace them with your own.

## The method

### The subscribe offer (earn the email)
An email address is a real ask. The subscribe offer is the promise that makes it worth it: a specific, recurring value the reader cannot get by scrolling. "Sign up for updates" earns nothing. "Every week, one teardown of how a real team does X" earns the click. Optionally pair it with a lead magnet, but the durable offer is the promise of every issue, not a one-time download.

### Growth loops (compounding, not just paid)
One-time pushes plateau. Loops compound. Build the loops that fit the topic: a referral mechanic (readers invite readers for a reward), shareable issues (a format people forward), cross-promotions with adjacent newsletters, and a public archive that pulls search traffic. Name the two or three loops worth building, because a loop beats a blast every time.

### Content rhythm (keep them opening)
Consistency is the retention engine. Set a cadence you can actually hold and a recognizable structure so readers know what they are getting. The issue people open every week is worth more than the perfect issue that shows up randomly. Define the recurring format so writing gets easier and opens stay high.

### Activation (the first two weeks decide)
Most unsubscribes happen right after signup, when the promise and the reality first meet. Plan the welcome sequence and the first few issues to deliver the offer immediately, so a new subscriber gets the value they were promised before they forget why they signed up. Acquisition without activation is a leaky bucket.

### Metrics that matter (not list size)
List size is a vanity number. A big, dead list is worse than a small, engaged one. Track the NORTH_STAR of engaged subscribers (opens and clicks over time), net growth (subscribes minus unsubscribes), the growth-loop contribution (how many came from readers, not spend), and, where the CRM allows, pipeline touched. The skill flags when someone is optimizing for a number that does not pay.

## Quality gates
- The subscribe offer is a specific recurring promise, not "get updates."
- At least one compounding growth loop is in the plan, not only paid or one-time pushes.
- Activation (welcome plus first issues) is planned, not just acquisition.
- The headline metric is engagement, not raw list size, and the skill says so out loud.
- Any rate or count shown is labeled illustrative unless it came from your connected data.

## Output (example)
```
NEWSLETTER GROWTH PLAN · weekly teardown newsletter (illustrative)
Topic: one real team's workflow, taken apart, every Thursday.

Subscribe offer: "Every Thursday, one teardown you can copy by Friday."
  (plus optional lead magnet: a starter pack of the 5 best past teardowns)

Growth loops:
  1. Referral: invite 3, unlock the private archive
  2. Shareable format: each issue ends with a copy-paste checklist people forward
  3. Cross-promo: swap mentions with 2 adjacent newsletters each month

Rhythm: same 3-part structure weekly (the setup, the teardown, the steal).

Activation: 1 welcome + best-of issue within 24h, so the promise lands before they forget.

Metrics (north star = engaged subs):
  Engaged (opened 2 of last 4)   track weekly
  Net growth (subs - unsubs)      not gross signups
  % from loops vs paid            is growth compounding?
  Pipeline touched (via CRM)      business impact, not applause

Next move: connect the email tool so the plan targets your real bottleneck, not a generic one.
```

## Where the inputs come from
The topic, audience, cadence, and offer come from you. Real opens, clicks, unsubscribes, and the growth curve come from an email or newsletter tool. Signup sources and referrers come from a web-analytics tool. Pipeline impact comes from a CRM. Every rate in the plan is illustrative until grounded in your own connected numbers, and the skill says which is which.
