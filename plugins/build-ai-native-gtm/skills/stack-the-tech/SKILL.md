# Stack the Tech: buy the plumbing, build the edge

## What this does
Turns "should we buy a tool or build something" into a per-step decision for one workflow. You stop rebuilding infrastructure and stop buying a platform to solve a problem you could build the thin edge for.

## What you'll need
One workflow, broken into its actual steps (the output of Solve the Problem). No connectors required.

## The method
1. Break the workflow into steps, end to end. For a lead handoff: enrich, score, route, research, draft the first touch, log, follow up.
2. Tag each step buy, build, or have. Buy what is commodity plumbing you would be crazy to rebuild (CRM, dialer, email). Have is what you already own. Build is the rest.
3. Buy the plumbing. Do not rebuild the CRM. Do not rebuild the dialer. Infrastructure is bought.
4. Build only the edge. The thin layer that is actually yours: the logic, the app on top, the view, the rules. That is where your advantage lives, and it is smaller than you think.
5. Decide per workflow, not per vendor pitch. A tool that fits this workflow may be wrong for the next one. The unit is the workflow, not the contract.

## Failure points
- Rebuilding infrastructure because building feels productive.
- Buying a platform to avoid building the small edge that is the actual advantage.
- Letting a vendor define the workflow so their tool fits it.
